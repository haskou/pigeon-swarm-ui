# Device sealed secrets (client)

Client side of haskou/pigeon-swarm-node#451. Design: `docs/design/device-sealed-secrets.md` in the node repo. Module: `src/contexts/identities/`.

## Scope

- Seal, open, publish (`PUT`), and fetch the caller's own entry (`GET`).
- Wired into the app. Unlock opens the device-sealed root (`PigeonIdentitySessionApi`) and fails closed when none opens. Mailbox readers, the authorship tag (`DirectMessageAuthorship.tag`), drafts and the keychain cipher (`KeychainCipher`) derive from `sessionRootKey(session)`, which throws without an open root. The private blob upload ledger (`PrivateBlobUploadLedger`) and the pending recovery records are sealed to the device credential (`deviceCredentialKeyPair`). The node has no re-encryption endpoint for the keychain, so the client re-seals it under the root.
- Creation mints the root. `PigeonIdentityRepository.create` opens it through `PigeonIdentitiesGateway.openDeviceSealedRoot` (`DeviceSealedRootRotation.current`) before it registers the session, so the first keychain write (for example, the first conversation) has a root under #320.

## Seal

- One fresh payload per generation, sealed to every recipient device credential with `PublicKey.encrypt`. The design doc says "fresh payload per credential"; this client uses one payload across recipients because every device must derive the same mailbox root from it. Each entry is still sealed separately to its recipient.
- Each entry is signed by the publishing device key over the preimage built by `DeviceSealedSecretSignature` from `@haskou/pigeon-swarm-crypto`: canonical JSON with alphabetical keys `authorizationRevision`, `generation`, `identityId`, `publisherCredential`, `purpose` (`pigeon:device-sealed-secret:v1`), `recipientCredential`, `sealedSecret`, the same bytes as the node's `DeviceSealedSecretBundle.signingPayload`. Credentials are the body-only base64 form, without PEM headers, both in the preimage and in the entries. The library refuses to sign with a key other than `publisherCredential`. `DeviceSealedSecretCipher.spec.ts` checks the library's `vectors/device-sealed-secret.json`.
- The client checks 1 to 128 recipients. The node also rejects a `sealedSecret` longer than 4096 characters with `400`; the client does not check that length.

## Publish

- `PUT /identity-devices/{identityId}/sealed-secrets` with device proof headers only (`X-Timestamp`, `X-Device-Credential`, `X-Device-Signature`). Body: `{ generation, authorizationRevision, entries }`, where `authorizationRevision` is the session revision.
- The ack must echo `identityId`, `generation`, and `authorizationRevision`. Any other ack throws.
- A `409` is not retried here. The caller refetches the head and rebuilds the publication.

## Rotation

- Revoke: `PigeonIdentitiesGateway.revokeDevice` reads the keychain head under the current root and records that root, sealed to the device credential (`deviceCredentialKeyPair`), before the revoke (`DeviceSealedRecovery.rememberKeychainRewrap`). It then revokes, publishes a fresh root to the remaining devices with `DeviceSealedRootRotation.rotate`, and rewraps the head under the new root (`rewrapAfterRevoke`). If the record cannot be written, the revoke does not run. If the rewrap fails after the revoke has applied, the record stays and unlock retries it.
- Revoke and mailboxes (#311, #405): `rotate` returns the session with the replaced root as `previousDeviceSealedRootKey`, held in memory only. The device dialog stores that session, then awaits the cut-off (`DirectMessageMailbox.cutOffRevokedReaders` and `GroupConversationMailbox.cutOffRevokedReaders`, run together) before it reports the revoke done; until then it keeps the revoked device listed, even though the stored session already re-renders it. In every one-to-one inbox and every readable generation inbox of this identity's groups, the cut-off registers the new root reader through the replaced root and prunes the replaced root's readers, so the revoked device cannot read records written after it. A failed cut-off is shown and retried by the next sync of that inbox. Residuals: see `opaque-mailboxes-client.md`, Residual risk.
- Revoke carries the replaced root (#376): `rotate` publishes `<root>.<previous root>` (`DeviceSealedRootPayload`), sealed only to the devices listed at the revoke's revision, so the revoked device never gets an entry. Every recipient already held the previous root. A remaining device opens it with `current`, which returns the previous root as `previousDeviceSealedRootKey`: at first access its local state sealed under the previous root (MLS state, plaintext cache, group generations; see `opaque-mailboxes-client.md`, Local state sealing) is re-sealed under the new root, and its one-to-one and group inboxes bridge as after a forget. A 409 retry publishes at the head revision, so a device enrolled between the revoke and the retry also receives the previous root. Enrolment (`reseal`) publishes `<root>` only.
- Unlock: `current` runs at the end of `unlock`. It reads the device's own entry when the authorization revision matches. A stale session re-syncs once and reads the entry again. A device without an entry publishes. It reads the identity keychain head first. A head that does not open fails that step, so no root is minted over it. No head publishes. An entry still behind the head after the re-sync means the head moved without a new generation, as after a forget whose hand-over was not stored (see Forget), or a revoke whose rotate failed. `DeviceSealedRecovery.rotateLaggingRoot` then reads the keychain head under that lagging root (`DeviceSealedRootRotation.laggingRoot`), records the root sealed to the device credential as a revoke does, publishes a fresh root with `rotate` from the lagging root, and rewraps the head under it. The rotated session keeps the lagging root as `previousDeviceSealedRootKey`, so the mailboxes bridge from it. The lagging root is never resealed. Its publisher was revoked since in both cases, so it opens only when it is the root this device trusted (see Fetch); a root it never trusted reads no head and mints nothing. A head that does not open under it, or a failed publish, mints nothing, and the next unlock tries again. Pending recovery then runs: `resumeRootReseal` after `synchronize` reseals a recorded pairing root at the synchronized revision; once the root is open, `resumeKeychainRewrap` rewraps a head that a revoke or a lagging rotation left under the previous root, and `rewrapHandedOverKeychain` moves a head still under the root a hand-over replaced. A record is kept while the step fails, and dropped on success, or when its revision no longer matches.
- Recovery-key sign-in (#464): `PigeonIdentitiesGateway.recover` calls `DeviceSealedRootRotation.openAfterRecovery`, never `current`, and only after `PigeonDeviceAuthorizationApi.recover` proved the recovery key and submitted the recovery transition. The recovery replaced every device, so no authorized device holds the root the keychain head is sealed under. `openAfterRecovery` replays the signed transitions (`verifiedDevices`) and refuses unless this device is the only one authorized. It generates a fresh root and always replaces an existing head, which cannot open under that root, with an empty keychain chained to it (`PigeonKeychainApi.replaceHeadAfterRecovery`), and then publishes the root sealed to that verified device only, not to the node's device list. A failed replacement publishes no root; a failed publish leaves the head under a root no one holds, and the next recovery replaces it again. Unlock and pairing keep the no-entry guard above: they never replace a head, so a corrupt or withheld head only fails them. The old conversation keys are lost; see `local-unlock.md`, Recovery-key sign-in.
- Unlock fails closed: if no device-sealed root is open at the end, unlock and recovery throw `deviceRootUnavailable` and no session is registered. `sessionRootKey` throws without a root, so nothing derives from the identity root (`masterKey`). Drafts are sealed under the device-sealed root; a draft that does not decrypt is skipped. Conversation drafts on the node are also keyed by an HMAC under a key derived from that root (`ConversationDraftCipher`; see `opaque-mailboxes-client.md`, Drafts), so a fresh root leaves the old drafts unreadable and unaddressed until the node evicts them.
- Enrolment keeps the root. `PigeonIdentitiesGateway.authorizeDevicePairing` first asks again for the device password (`confirmLocalUnlock`, no side effects); a refusal publishes nothing and authorizes nothing. It then opens the root when the session holds none (`DeviceSealedRootRotation.current`), and refuses if no root results. An unreadable keychain head fails that open, so no root is minted over it. After the authorization it enrols the new device's mailbox reader in every one-to-one inbox (`DevicePairingReaders.enrol`; see `opaque-mailboxes-client.md`, Reader set), best effort. It then records the root sealed to the device credential (`DeviceSealedRecovery.resealAfterPairing`). `DeviceSealedRootRotation.reseal` publishes the same root at the new revision to every device, so the keychain stays readable. A failed reseal keeps the record, and unlock retries it. The completion code is returned either way. Only a revoke, or the unlock of a device whose entry lags the head, mints a fresh root. The root is shared across recipients, so every device derives the same mailbox root from it. This deviates from the node design's per-credential payload; the node side should confirm it accepts one payload across recipients.
- Completion opens the root before the workspace. `PigeonIdentitiesGateway.completeDevicePairing` calls `DeviceSealedRootRotation.current` on the paired session, refuses the pairing if no root results, then hydrates the keychain and imports the pairing history under that root (#356).
- Concurrent publishes: the later generation wins. A device that lost the race re-enrols on its next unlock. Two remaining devices that unlock at once after a forget both rotate; each rewraps the keychain head under its own root, so the head can end under the root that lost. That is the same race as two concurrent revokes.
- Residuals (#300):
  - Pairing: if the authorizer's reseal failed, the new device finds no root entry and an unreadable keychain head, so its completion is refused until the authorizer's next unlock retries the reseal. If the pending record also cannot be written, nothing retries the reseal. The device stays authorized, and the completion code was still returned. Revoke it and pair again.
  - Pending records are stored per identity in localStorage (`scopeClientStorageKey`, `device-sealed-recovery:v1:<identityId>`). Their root copies are sealed to this device's credential (`deviceCredentialKeyPair`), so they are not plaintext. An encrypted copy of the root still stays at rest until its step clears. Clearing browser storage loses pending records, and nothing retries those steps. A record that no longer opens under the current credential is dropped.
  - Recovery runs only on the device that recorded it. If that device never unlocks again, the step stays pending. Survivors then cannot open the keychain until a device that still holds the previous root republishes it. A device that proves at sign-in, from the signed transitions, that it was removed discards them with the rest of its local copy (`DeviceSealedRecovery.discard`, via `eraseRemovedDevice`), because it can no longer run them.
  - A revoke whose rotate fails after the revoke applied leaves a stale own entry holding the old root. Recovery does not reseal that root, because a compromised root could be republished. The next unlock of a remaining device that trusted the old root mints a fresh root instead (`rotateLaggingRoot`), sealed to every remaining device.
- No-entry guard residual: a device whose head read fails at its first unlock keeps no root, so its unlock fails closed until a device that holds the root reseals it.
- Forget (#310): the identities use case `CurrentDeviceForgetter` retires the forgetting device through `OwnDeviceRepository` (`retireOwnDevice` on the node) unless it is the only device. `PigeonDeviceAuthorizationApi.prepareOwnRetirement` builds the self-revocation and lists the remaining devices, and `DeviceSealedRootRotation.handOver` mints a fresh root in memory and seals it to the remaining devices only, signed by the forgetter, at the revocation revision and the next generation (see Generation). The sealed payload is `<root>.<previous root>` in base64url (`DeviceSealedRootPayload`); a plain publish carries `<root>` only, and any other shape is refused. The previous root is the one the recipients already hold, so it exposes nothing new. The generation rides in the revocation (`POST /identity-devices/transitions`, field `sealedSecrets`, node `haskou/pigeon-swarm-node#289`): the node applies both or neither. Its response carries `sealedSecrets: { authorizationRevision, generation }` only when it stored the generation; then the forgetter rewraps the keychain head under the fresh root (identity-signed, so it lands after the revoke), best effort. Without that confirmation, as when a concurrent change won the race, the forgetter does not move the head, and the first remaining device to unlock rotates through `rotateLaggingRoot` (see Unlock). A hand-over that cannot be prepared falls back the same way, including when this device's own entry is a hand-over superseded by a root it trusts (`SupersededDeviceSealedRootHandOverError`; see Hand-over order): `retireOwnDevice` logs it, retires without a hand-over and leaves the head where it is. A remaining device opens the hand-over with `current`, which returns the previous root as `previousDeviceSealedRootKey`; unlock rewraps a head still under it (`rewrapHandedOverKeychain`, a no-op once moved), and the mailboxes bridge and prune from it. While the hand-over generation is current, each unlock reads the head once more for that check. The mailbox readers of the forgetter are then released (not for the only device) and the local unlock is dropped. Exposure window: none for the root after a stored hand-over; the old root's mailbox readers stay until a remaining device bridges (see `opaque-mailboxes-client.md`, Residual risk).
- Multi-rotation residual: a device that missed several generations bridges each inbox on its next open. Until it bridges, that inbox stays unread on the device; the previous root's readers are pruned as soon as the credential list no longer holds them (see `opaque-mailboxes-client.md`, Pruning). Its local state opens only under a root it can name: a device that missed one rotation re-seals it from the previous root; one that missed two, or one followed by an enrolment, holds neither root, so its sealed MLS state, plaintext cache and group generations are dropped at first access. Its groups must add it again, and its communities need a fresh key package and welcome.

## Fetch

- `GET` with device proof. Returns the caller's own entry of the stored generation, opened with the device key. Returns `null` on `404`, meaning no entry exists for this device yet.
- Opening checks that the identity and recipient match, verifies the publisher signature over the preimage, then decrypts, and returns the publisher credential that signature verified under.
- Publisher (#532, #537): the secret API decrypts an entry before any check, but `DeviceSealedRootRotation` neither uses nor trusts its root or its generation (but see Trusted root) until `PigeonDeviceAuthorizationApi.verifyPublisher` checks that publisher against the signed device transitions, replayed from the identity record as `synchronize` does (`DeviceRemovalDomainService.verifiedReplay`). `DeviceSealedSecretPublisherDomainService` accepts a plain publish only from a device the state at the entry's `authorizationRevision` authorizes and every later state still authorizes. A device revoked later, by another device or by itself, is refused (`RevokedDeviceSealedSecretPublisherError`): its key may be in other hands and could sign an entry naming any revision it once held. It accepts a hand-over only from a device the state before that revision authorized and whose own self-revocation, accepted by the replay, produced that revision. Any other publisher is refused (`UnauthorizedDeviceSealedSecretPublisherError`), so a key the node mints opens nothing however validly it signs. The check fails closed: transitions that are not served or do not prove this device, and a revision the replay never reached (an entry from before a recovery), refuse too. The call that needed the entry then rejects without a root, so unlock fails with `deviceRootUnavailable` and no local device-sealed row is re-sealed or wiped. Only the entry the device opens is checked: a stale entry that `current` replaces after re-syncing is never opened.
- Hand-over order (#539): the replay cannot tell the genuine hand-over from another one the retired key signs for the same revision, so `verifyPublisher` returns whether the publisher is `authorized` or `retired` (`DeviceSealedSecretPublisherStanding`). The genuine hand-over rides in the retirement, so it is the first entry at its revision. A hand-over at or below the revision of another root this device trusts (see Trusted root) is refused (`SupersededDeviceSealedRootHandOverError`), by `current`, `laggingRoot`, `handOver` and a publish alike, so a retired key with a colluding node cannot roll a device back to a root of its choice once that device moved past the hand-over. A plain publish at that revision is not ordered this way: an authorized device may rotate there. A hand-over that opens has its generation bounded to a day past the clock, as a revoked entry's is (see Generation), since the retired key could sign any generation (#541).
- Replay cost (#539): every check fetches the transition log and replays it from the identity record, once per checked entry. One unlock checks up to three times (`current`, then `storedGeneration` and the entry checks of `rotateLaggingRoot`), so its cost grows with the log. Sharing one replay per call is not done yet.
- Trusted root (#537, #539, #541): a revoke whose rotate never landed, or a forget whose hand-over lost the race, leaves the remaining devices an entry from a publisher revoked since. So that they are not stuck, `DeviceSealedRootTrustStore` keeps, per identity, the authorization revision and an HMAC-SHA256 fingerprint of the one root this device last opened from an entry that passed the check or published itself; never the root. The record is `<revision>.<revision tag>.<fingerprint>`: the revision tag is an HMAC-SHA256 over `pigeon-swarm/device-sealed-root-trust/revision:<identity>:<revision>`, so the revision is authenticated, and the fingerprint covers the identity, the revision and the root. Both are keyed by an HKDF-SHA256 subkey (`pigeon-swarm/device-sealed-root-trust/v3`) of the session `masterKey`, never by the root, so a record or revision written to storage without that key is no record: it trusts and supersedes nothing. The kept revision never lowers: remembering a root at a lower revision than the authenticated one kept keeps the higher revision, so a node rolling an entry back cannot un-supersede a hand-over. That key is the vault root key every paired device of the identity shares, not a key of this device alone, so it is never used as the HMAC key itself. The storage key is versioned (`device-sealed-root-trust:v3`); a record of an earlier version is not read and trusts nothing. The `v1` and `v2` keys of an identity are removed, without migration, whenever its record is written or forgotten. Such an entry is used only while its root matches that fingerprint, and only to move off it (`laggingRoot`, `handOver`): `current` never adopts its root or its generation, and returns the synchronized session without a root, so unlock rotates off it through `rotateLaggingRoot`. Its generation, bounded (see Generation), still orders the next publish, since the node refuses a generation not above the stored one. A device that never trusted that root fails closed with `deviceRootUnavailable`, reads no keychain head under it, mints nothing and keeps every local row, until a device that trusted it unlocks and seals a fresh root to every remaining device, or the device is paired again. A device that does trust it keeps failing unlock with `deviceRootUnavailable` for as long as its rotate off that root keeps failing (offline, `409`, unreadable head), since `current` never adopts the revoked root. `DeviceSealedRecovery.discard` forgets the fingerprint with the rest.
- Silent trust write (#539): remembering the fingerprint never fails the call; a refused storage write is only logged. A device whose write failed, and whose next revoke's rotate then fails too, holds no fingerprint for the root it is left on, so it waits, like a device that never trusted that root, for a trusting device to rotate, or is paired again. Without a fingerprint it also orders no hand-over (see Hand-over residual).

## Generation

- `max(stored generation, clock) + 1`. A device without its own entry cannot read the stored generation, so the clock keeps the next generation ahead of a stale head. Resolved in `DeviceSealedRootRotation`.
- The generation is authenticated with the sealed secret: the publisher signature covers both (see Seal), so a node cannot move an entry to another generation without a new signature. The publisher is verified against the signed device transitions before the generation is used (see Fetch), so a node cannot mint its own publisher key to plant a root or a generation of its choice.
- Revoked-publisher residual (#537, #539): a node holding the key of a device revoked later can still plant an entry naming a revision that device held, but no remaining device uses its root unless it is the root that device already trusted, which the revoked device held anyway, and then only to rotate off it. Its generation is still read to publish above it, bounded to a day past the clock: `max(min(stored, clock + 1 day), clock) + 1`, so a forged generation near `MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` never makes this device publish or hand over at one, and is never stamped on a session as `deviceSealedRootGeneration`. The node keeps no upper bound on a generation, so a node that stored such a generation refuses the bounded publish with `409`: publishes fail closed, nothing opens, until the node bounds generations too. The bound is relative to this device's clock: a clock more than a day behind the time a revoked or retired generation was stored computes a bounded generation at or below the stored one, so the publish also gets `409` and fails closed until the clock is corrected. Devices that were already behind a revoked publisher before this check shipped trust no root yet, and wait for a device that does, or pair again. Writing the generation into the sealed payload as well would add nothing.
- Hand-over residual (#536, #539): a hand-over is ordered only against the root this device trusts. A device that trusts no root at or above the hand-over revision, because it was offline from before the retirement until the node serves the entry, or because its trust write failed, still opens another hand-over the retired key signs for that revision with a colluding node, and adopts and remembers its root. The retired device knew the genuine hand-over root too, since it minted it, so this exposes no root that the genuine hand-over did not; a remaining device that rotates moves every device off it.
- Stores order roots by this generation (#524). A node that restores an older head and serves a lower generation makes a live device's stores refuse that root, keeping their rows, until the device forgets them.
- A canary record renewed by a session without a generation drops its stamp; it does not keep the stored one. Every session that reaches a store gets its generation from `DeviceSealedRootRotation` (`current` or a publish), so a session without one is a session from before the generation was carried. Keeping a stamp it cannot order against would refuse a genuine session published at that generation. Dropping it means the stores wipe, never lock out, until a session with a generation stamps the record again.

## Tests

- The cipher and API specs use real keys, so they are listed in `REAL_CRYPTO_SPECS` in `jest.config.cjs` and run in the `crypto` project (`yarn test:crypto`), like `PigeonDeviceAuthorizationApi.spec.ts`. `DeviceSealedRootRotation.spec.ts` stubs its collaborators but runs in the `crypto` project too, with `DeviceSealedRootTrustStore.spec.ts`, because the trust fingerprint is a real HMAC.
- Forget rotation (#310): the red-first unlock test in `PigeonIdentitySessionApi.spec.ts` wires the real `DeviceSealedRootRotation` and `DeviceSealedRecovery` over mocked node APIs. It checks that an entry behind the head after a forget publishes a fresh root at the head revision and rewraps the keychain head under it. `DeviceSealedRecovery.spec.ts` covers the no-lag, unreadable-head, failed-publish, and no-head cases; `DeviceSealedRootRotation.spec.ts` covers `laggingRoot`.
- Revoked publisher (#537): red-first, `PigeonDeviceAuthorizationApi.spec.ts` replays real signed transitions and refuses a publisher another device revoked later, or that retired itself after its entry, at a revision that still authorized it, while still accepting a publisher the head authorizes and a hand-over. `DeviceSealedRootRotation.spec.ts` covers the trusted-root fallback, the generation of a revoked entry and the per-identity trust; `PigeonIdentitySessionApi.spec.ts` checks over the real rotation and recovery that an untrusted lagging root fails the unlock without reading the head or publishing, and that a trusted one rotates and rewraps the keychain head; `DeviceSealedRootTrustStore.spec.ts` covers the keyed fingerprint.
- Bounds and order (#539): red-first, `PigeonDeviceAuthorizationApi.spec.ts` checks that `verifyPublisher` reports a hand-over as `retired` and any other accepted publisher as `authorized`. `DeviceSealedRootRotation.spec.ts` checks that a revoked entry's generation near `MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` makes neither a publish nor a hand-over go past a day after the clock, that `current` on a stale session adopts neither its root nor its generation and leaves it to `laggingRoot`, and that a hand-over at or below the revision of another trusted root is refused everywhere while a first hand-over and a later plain entry at its revision still open. `DeviceSealedRootTrustStore.spec.ts` pins the fingerprint to the HKDF subkey, never the vault root key, and checks that an unparseable or earlier record trusts and supersedes nothing.
- Trust record (#541): red-first, `DeviceSealedRootTrustStore.spec.ts` pins the revision tag and the fingerprint to the `v3` HKDF subkey, checks that a record without the tag or with a revision it cannot authenticate trusts and supersedes nothing and is not kept, that the kept revision never lowers, and that the `v1` and `v2` keys of the identity, and only those, are removed on remember and forget. `DeviceSealedRootRotation.spec.ts` checks that a hand-over generation near `MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` is bounded in `current` and in the next publish, and that a hand-over at the trusted revision is still refused after the node rolled back to a lower plain entry. `PigeonIdentitiesGateway.spec.ts` pins that a superseded own hand-over retires without a hand-over or a rewrap; it passed before the fix, as a pin.
- Shared vectors: `DeviceAuthorizationTransition.vectors.spec.ts` and `KeychainCipher.vectors.spec.ts` load `@haskou/pigeon-swarm-crypto/vectors/device-authorization-transition.json` and `@haskou/pigeon-swarm-crypto/vectors/keychain-signature.json`, the vectors the node also checks. The transition spec builds enrollment (direct and from the new device's proof), plain and compromised revocation, and recovery with and without caps (given unsorted) from the vector keys and expects the proof, signature and body byte for byte. The keychain spec pins the signed keychain payload (`pigeon:keychain:v1\n` domain; `previousKeychainExternalIdentifier` omitted on the first version) and its signature.
